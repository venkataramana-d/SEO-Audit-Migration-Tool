1. Before you start

4–6 weeks before

Write down how the old site is performing today, so you can prove what changed later.

Save 12 months of Google Search Console data
Critical
Why: Your "before" picture — without it you can't tell how much traffic you lost or gained. How: Export clicks, impressions and rankings from Search Console before you touch anything. (The Site Audit tab already pulls this via GSC.)
Crawl the entire old website
Critical
Why: A full list of every page that must be accounted for on the new site — nothing gets forgotten. How: Use the Site Audit tab (or Screaming Frog) to list every URL, title, and status.
List your most important pages
High
Why: Your top-traffic and most-linked pages are the ones you can least afford to lose. How: The Site Audit tab flags ★ TOP pages by GSC clicks — that's your "do not lose" list.
Note current rankings for key search terms
Standard
Why: Rankings move around normally — a clear "before" number tells you if a later drop is really the migration. How: Save current positions for your most important keywords.

2. Plan the move

3–4 weeks before

The most important phase. Getting this right is what actually protects your rankings.

Confirm every URL stays exactly the same
Critical
Why: You are keeping the same URLs — this is the lowest-risk migration, because Google needs no re-mapping. But it only holds if URLs are truly identical. How: Compare this tool's crawled URL list against the new build; every path must match exactly.
Watch for accidental URL changes (slash, case, parameters)
Critical
Why: Replatforms often silently change a trailing slash, letter case, or add tracking parameters — Google treats those as different URLs and rankings drop. How: Spot-check that /in/page/ stays /in/page/ (not /in/page or /in/Page).
Add a 301 redirect only for any URL that must change
High
Why: Since URLs stay the same, you generally need no redirects — but if a few must change, a 301 preserves their ranking value. How: Redirect just those specific URLs on the server (one hop, no chains). Everything else keeps its URL.
Carry over structured data (schema markup)
High
Why: This gives you star ratings, FAQs and other rich results. Losing it is an immediate, visible downgrade. How: List what schema each page type has now, and require the new site to include the same.
Pick a low-risk launch date
Standard
Why: If something breaks, you want time and attention to fix it — not during your busiest sales period. How: Launch on a quiet day, early in the week, when your team can watch closely.

3. Build & test

2–3 weeks before

Catch problems on the test site, where fixing them is free — not on the live site, where it costs traffic.

Block the test (staging) site from Google
Critical
Why: If Google indexes your unfinished test site, it competes with your real site and confuses rankings. How: Password-protect staging — don't rely on robots.txt alone.
Check the new site works well on mobile
Critical
Why: Google mainly looks at the mobile version to decide rankings. How: Compare mobile and desktop of key pages — make sure nothing important is missing on mobile.
Move over titles, descriptions and headings
High
Why: Some of the most direct signals Google uses per page. Losing them resets years of work. How: Import your saved titles/descriptions into the new pages, then spot-check a sample.
Test site speed on the new templates
High
Why: A slower new site can rank worse than the old one, even with identical content. How: Run each page type through Google PageSpeed Insights and fix anything flagged red.
Prepare the new sitemap.xml
Standard
Why: A ready sitemap helps Google find and re-crawl all new pages faster after launch. How: Generate it from the final new-site URL list only.
Verify every URL still resolves (no new 404s)
Critical
Why: Because URLs stay the same, the key risk is a page silently going missing on the new build. How: Run the full crawled URL list against staging; every URL must return 200, not 404 — this tool's audit does exactly that.

4. Launch day

Go-live

Follow this order exactly — most launch-day damage comes from doing these steps out of sequence.

Remove any "block Google" settings from the new site
Critical
Why: If the live site is still blocked, Google's first visit hits a dead end and delays everything. How: Turn off password protection and noindex tags right before going live, and confirm it took effect.
Confirm all URLs load on the live site
Critical
Why: URLs stay the same, so the launch-day risk is a page or section failing to load rather than a redirect. How: Immediately after go-live, re-crawl and confirm no 404s or 5xx errors appeared.
Confirm HTTPS (the padlock) works
Critical
Why: A broken certificate blocks visitors and Google alike and shows a scary warning. How: Load the new site and check for the padlock with no warnings.
Confirm analytics tracking is working
High
Why: If tracking is broken you lose the data needed to spot problems in the critical first days. How: Load the live site and check it appears in Google Analytics real-time view.
Submit the sitemap & request re-crawl in Search Console
Critical
Why: Same domain and URLs, so no "Change of Address" is needed — but you still want Google to re-crawl the new pages quickly. How: Submit the (unchanged) sitemap and use URL Inspection → Request Indexing on your top pages.
Manually check your most important pages
Critical
Why: Your last checkpoint before real visitors and Google see any mistakes. How: Click through your "do not lose" list plus a random sample of other pages on the live site.
